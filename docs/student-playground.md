# Student playground

Open `/playground`, or choose **Student Playground** in Other Features. **Schale Residence Hall** has the supplied S.C.H.A.L.E. asset with seven furnished rooms and up to 21 students, free roaming, coordinated greetings, direct student interaction, pause, camera controls, and PNG photos. A startup loading screen covers both catalog loading and scene preparation until the hall, every initial student model and its first animation are ready. Failed models can be retried or skipped; the entire student catalog is not preloaded.

The environment uses the user-supplied OBJ/PNG files from `Development_data/S.C.H.A.L.E`, matching the visual reference of [S.C.H.A.L.E. by Mi_kuNeko_](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/schale-fec59af02c45477d89053f2451aacf22). The converted scene is `public/assets/playground/schale-office.glb`: 93 unique meshes, 34,948 triangles and approximately 2.3 MiB including embedded textures capped at 1024 pixels. The source has 135 meshes and 76 PNGs but no MTL/material bindings; assignments, transparency and lighting were reconstructed from the textures and supplied screenshot, so shading is approximate. The conversion removes exact duplicate meshes, zero-area triangles and malformed joins between glass panes. Originals are untouched. `schale-office.source.json` records the conversion and recovered assignments.

The seven occupancy sections follow the actual lounge, shop, meeting room, gym, game room, café and changing room. `playground-navigation.json` contains a 1/16-unit clearance field baked from the shipped scene by `scripts/bake-playground-clearance.py`. It is the single floor-clearance check; legacy coarse floor rows do not add a second margin. Walls, perimeter rails, windows, door frames and sealed shafts retain their measured boundaries. Furniture has a 0.30-unit clearance allowance calculated from signed distance, letting clothing/equipment overlap edges and use narrow garden/storage aisles while benches, cabinets, tables and treadmill centers still block movement. Door panes and the gym sliding leaves' shared center trim are passable; outer door jambs and windows remain solid. Playground students normalize to a 1.5-unit standing height, measured from visible Body/Face/Hair material primitives in the reviewed Walk pose before source units are scaled. The Walk clip is prepared during model loading, sampled once, then the original Idle pose and its bounds are restored. Crouched or seated Idle poses, such as Yuzu (Maid) inside her box, retain their authored proportions instead of enlarging the whole model. Halos, separate equipment, hidden bodies and generated outline children do not control their height. Every student uses the same 0.35-unit walking radius, independent of model width, hair, equipment, source units or arrangement scale. Visible model height and proportions stay unchanged; hair, clothing and equipment may overlap in narrow spaces. Manual walking retains the smaller student contact cores, and autonomous students keep comfortable spacing. Loading checks relocate an unsafe or disconnected spawn to connected clear floor. The scene loads as one local GLB with a versioned URL to invalidate older cached conversions; student movement and visibility wait until it is ready.

Playground clip-start placement refreshes the holder's world and skin bind transforms before measuring the new pose. This keeps a newly normalized model above the floor on its first animation, instead of using the previous source-unit bind transform. `/3D` and Photo Studio retain their existing preview sizing.

Each room starts with a random placement target of one to three unique students with reviewed Walk animations. These are population preferences, not room quotas: students can enter, walk, be carried and chat in any room with clear floor, even when more than three students are there. The initial mix includes all three population sizes; 21 is the hall-wide maximum, not a fixed population. Every 30–50 active seconds one room changes its target. Five-second roster checks compare the total visitors with the sum of these targets, capped at 21, and add visitors or send surplus idle visitors home. A crowded room alone never forces a departure. Selected and Stay here students are protected, and encounters finish before departure. Manual invitations can raise the desired population while a total visitor slot is available. Variants share one identity, including while loading or departing: Akane and Akane (School) cannot visit together.

Visitors also rotate after at least 45 active seconds. Departing visitors walk to the entrance and fade over 1.5 seconds. A blocked route waits and replans; idle roaming friends step aside when they block a departure. Stay here students hold their position. A departing student's slot and identity remain reserved until the fade ends. Invitations use other free slots immediately, and users can send multiple chosen students home. At full capacity, a swap requires an explicit choice of who leaves, then the replacement loads after that slot opens. The initial roster starts in its rooms; subsequent visitors load at the entrance, wait for a clear doorway, then walk to a reserved destination. Models load two at a time. Hidden or paused time does not advance visits. The hall layout and visitor roster are temporary on reload.

Students automatically meet local friends in rooms or the hall, face one another, alternate animated talking dots, and respond with hearts, sparkles, notes, ideas or questions in the 3D scene. Chats have a minimum duration of five to eight seconds. Each student starts looping Idle immediately after their own one-shot reaction finishes, while staying reserved for the conversation. Both wait until the other reaction has finished and both Idle clips have loaded and started before their next action and 18–28-second social cooldown. Reaction timing starts after clip loading; longer clips have no fifteen-second cutoff. Playback completion also accounts for throttled animation updates, and one-shot reactions keep advancing when their model is offscreen or hidden in first person. Other hidden animations remain paused. Manual walking, carrying and sending home can still interrupt a reaction. Roaming students sometimes visit the hall and then return to their room. Partner selection favors students who have waited longer. Meetings require a clear line through the scenery before talking starts and throughout the conversation; a manual invitation across a wall searches for an accessible meeting spot. There are no floating names or text dialogue boxes. Click a model to open its actions, drag to carry it to clear floor, or select it and tap floor to walk. Room counts do not restrict movement, dragging, routes or conversations; physical scenery and student footprints still apply. Stay here stops autonomous movement; Explore resumes it.

Drag empty map space to pan with a mouse or one finger; student dragging still carries the student. Pickup faces the camera once, and carrying keeps that heading. Wheel and pinch zoom work during normal play. Right drag or two fingers also pan. Rotate enables drag-to-orbit and can be toggled off to return to normal play. Reset camera restores the complete hall overview. Invite shows collection images before selection and lets you explicitly invite or swap. **Residents** opens a lesson-style All Locations roster showing collection images in each student's current room, the hallway or the entrance. Room headings focus their area; Find focuses a student and opens their actions. Send home is available both on these cards and in model actions. Pause, reset camera and PNG photos remain in the compact HUD.

The Local clock uses the browser's current time and timezone, with the timezone shown in its tooltip. The sky blends through dawn (05:00–08:00), daytime (08:00–17:00), sunset (17:00–20:00) and night. The sky-only background contains the sun/moon, clouds and stars, with office lighting following the cycle. These are artistic local-hour phases, independent of the paused simulation. The clock ticks each second; the sky redraws once per minute, on resize or when returning to the page. One generated background texture is capped at 1024 pixels on its longest edge and is included in downloaded photos.

## Automatic animations and student camera

Select a student and choose **Student camera** to control them in a third-person follow view. WASD or arrow keys move relative to the current camera; desktop also has held direction buttons, while mobile has a drag joystick with diagonal movement. Release, pointer cancellation, pause, view changes, resizing and losing focus clear held input. A small center dead zone prevents unintended movement. The compact translucent camera panel leaves more of the scene visible, and the mobile title, local clock and icon toolbar share a single header that adapts to portrait and landscape. Drag the scene to orbit and scroll or pinch to zoom. Walk near another student and click **Talk to…** or press E to start a conversation with the existing animated talking effects. Moving interrupts the conversation. Exit or Escape restores the previous camera and the student's previous Explore / Stay here setting. Other students continue their autonomous activities.

Choose **First person** in the student camera panel to look from the student's eye level. Drag with a mouse or finger to look around; walking and nearby conversations keep the same controls and collision checks. The controlled model, its social effects and selection ring are hidden in this view so they cannot obstruct the camera. First person uses a fixed eye position with a wider view and no distance zoom. **Third person** restores the previous orbit angle and zoom around the student's current location. New control sessions start in third person; Exit also restores the overview lens and camera limits.

The playground always derives Idle, Walk, Reaction and Pickup from the imported student's available Idle, Walk, Touch and Pickup profile actions. Saved custom playground overrides are ignored; the custom editor and save endpoint have been removed. Newly imported profile actions appear on the next page load without a separate playground assignment. Unsupported actions stay disabled. Conversations use the imported Reaction or Idle animation with the 3D social effects; arbitrary clip names are not interpreted as greetings.

Direct movement checks the baked scenery clearance with the common student radius, without room-count restrictions. Students block manual walking only within a 0.5-unit center distance; existing overlaps can be escaped without moving deeper into another student's core. Small collision steps prevent tunneling. Input eases onto the actual wall tangent within a contact band and keeps sliding while touching the wall, avoiding alternating inward and sideways steps at high frame rates. The approach eases over a longer distance to handle tighter furniture corners; a clear forward step stays available when the projected tangent is negligible, so the assistance cannot create an extra invisible barrier. Walking away releases contact immediately. Student contact circles retain their tangent response, with bounded outward adjustment to avoid sticking on sampled collision-grid lips. Head-on input stops; tilted input slides. Heading turns gradually toward the actual movement. Nearby interactions require a clear line through scenery within 3.2 units; moving into range does not permit talking through walls. Third-person zoom stays between 4 and 18 units.

Rendering removes horizontal source travel and centers each clip's initial pose to keep its actor at the simulation position. Vertical motion and source expressions/equipment are retained. Movement starts after Walk loads. Legacy stored playground mappings remain compatible with record transfers, but have no effect on playback. No database purge or model rebuild is required.

## Deployment and checks

Run `npx prisma generate` and `npx prisma migrate deploy` before starting the updated application. The migration adds one JSON column without rebuilding models. Existing on-demand animation delivery and caching are reused.

The sandbox uses one canvas with a maximum of 21 actors, static imported office geometry and simple lighting, no dynamic shadow maps or postprocessing, bounded render pixel density, and reduced resolution after sustained slow frames. Departure fades use a screen-space shader discard that also covers custom color/depth materials without changing their normal transparency. Animation/simulation updates stop while the page is hidden; offscreen actors skip animation updates and effects. Nearby students update animation at 30 Hz and distant students at 15 Hz, retaining elapsed time. Model loading is limited to two concurrent students. Decision making runs once every two simulation seconds with bounded meeting attempts. Routes use A* on a quarter-unit office grid cached by walking radius, include other students' footprints, and skip the grid search for clear straight routes. Clear shortcuts and checked curved corners remove grid stair-steps; heading turns gradually along the route. Route segments check scenery every 0.02 units. Direct walking uses clearance checks without a per-frame path search. A blocked student switches to Idle, waits, and replans before walking again; idle roaming visitors can step aside, while Stay here visitors retain their position. Invalid drag/drop positions are rejected using the common student radius. Population and performance need testing on target mobile devices; a desktop result is not a mobile benchmark.

Run `npm run test:playground`. These tests cover GLB/material/texture integrity, nondegenerate scaled geometry, three valid spawn points and exit routes in every room, automatic basic roles, coordinated encounters, interrupt/release, direct walking and camera-relative input, movement limits, scenery clearance and stale animation requests. `src/lib/chibi/server.test.ts` also verifies imported roles override legacy settings. Run TypeScript, targeted ESLint and browser checks for desktop and touch controls.
